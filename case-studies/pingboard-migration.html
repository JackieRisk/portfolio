Migrating Pingboard into the Workleap Platform

Designing clarity and reassurance for customers moving onto the unified Workleap platform, one cohort at a time.

Role Senior Product Designer
Timeline Design began January 2025 for migrations starting April 2025, rolled out in phased customer cohorts
Team Product, Engineering, Product Marketing, Customer Success, and Platform teams, including Officevibe
Scope Customer journey, login and navigation redirects, integration re-authentication, relocated-feature guidance, communication and onboarding plans
Output Journey map, migration flows, redirect and error-handling experiences, onboarding and feature-discovery patterns
Tools Figma, Intercom

TL;DR

I designed the customer-facing experience for migrating existing Pingboard customers onto the unified Workleap platform, so they could keep their data and workflows intact while relocated features, new login URLs, and changed permissions took effect. I mapped the customer journey, ranked every change by whether customers had to act on it and how soon, designed the redirect and re-authentication experiences, and worked with Marketing and Customer Success on communications at 90, 30, and 7 days before migration and on migration day. The migration is now complete.

The Challenge

Pingboard customers were moving onto the unified Workleap platform to enable cross-product synergy and improve customer value. For the customer, that meant a lot of change at once.

Challenges & risks

  • Users had to reconnect integrations (HRIS, notifications, SSO).
  • Features were relocated across Workleap products (for example, Applause to Good Vibes, and Surveys and 1-on-1s to Officevibe).
  • The UX changed significantly: login URLs, user permissions, and deprecated features.
  • Confusion and frustration were real risks, given how much change customers had to manage.

What customers needed

  • Data integrity and minimal disruption.
  • Clear guidance for new workflows, with less friction.
  • A clear sense of the value of the migration.

Overview: a new "What's changed" nav item launches the welcome modal, and badges flag what moved. Emails keep customers informed at each step.

My Role & Scope

As Senior Product Designer, I owned the UX and interaction design and the user guidance and communication design for the migration, working across Product, Engineering, Customer Success, Marketing, and the platform and Officevibe teams:

  • Mapped the customer journey to understand where migration would create friction.
  • Prioritized clear UX, onboarding, and feature-discovery patterns.
  • Designed redirection and error-handling experiences, such as login redirects and integration re-authentication prompts.
  • Partnered with the platform and Officevibe teams to streamline the integration of cross-platform features.
  • Worked with Marketing and Customer Success on communication and onboarding plans at key milestones.
  • Planned feedback loops (Customer Success feedback and support tickets) to inform UX improvements.

Approach

1. Map the customer journey

I started by mapping the journey of an existing Pingboard admin being migrated, to see where friction would show up. The map separated what the customer had to do (reconnect integrations, confirm billing) from the "Where's my..." questions about features that had moved.

Customer journey map for a migrating admin, with prioritized steps and open questions.

2. Rank every change by what customers had to do

Not every change deserved the same attention. I mapped each change against each plan tier (Basic, Essential, and Pro) and rated it by whether the customer had to act and how soon: actionable and timely, actionable, FYI and timely, or FYI. Reconnecting integrations was the clearest "actionable and timely" change, so it came first in the flow.

Impacts to each plan, rated by urgency and whether the customer had to act.

3. Design for clarity and reassurance

The goal was for nothing to feel like it had disappeared or broken. That meant:

  • Seamless login and navigation. Redirects from the old URLs to the new pingboard.workleap.com address to avoid confusion, with clear messaging around SSO reactivation and password resets.
  • Feature guidance and education. Prompts and onboarding that explain relocated features, plus a "What's changed" nav item and badges that flag what moved.
  • Data migration transparency. Clear status and integrity messaging for migrated data such as teams, profiles, and groups.
  • Deprecated features. UX patterns for handling deprecated features and guiding people to new alternatives.
  • Consistency. Visual and interaction consistency, using Workleap's UI framework.

End-to-end migration flow, from the first email through relocated-feature guidance.

4. Communicate at every milestone

Transparency and communication were key. I worked with Marketing and Customer Success on communication and onboarding plans timed to 90, 30, and 7 days before migration, and on migration day, so customers were informed at every step.

5. Roll out in cohorts and learn as we go

Migrating in phased customer cohorts let us improve as we went. The bulk of cohorts were scheduled for April 1, 2025, and excluded companies with tricky situations that would have made for a poor experience. We planned feedback loops, drawing on Customer Success feedback and support tickets, to inform UX improvements between cohorts.

Results & Impact

  • The migration is complete: existing Pingboard customers moved onto the unified Workleap platform in phased cohorts.
  • Customers were guided through prioritized steps (integrations and billing first) rather than left to find their own way.
  • Designed the redirect, re-authentication, and "Where's my..." experiences that every cohort moved through.

Reflection

Early feedback from the first two cohorts pointed to five things that mattered most. These were early, anecdotal signals rather than measured results:

  • Proactive guidance and clarity. Users showed less friction when clearly guided through prioritized onboarding steps, such as integrations and billing.
  • Anticipating change-management challenges. Addressing likely points of confusion up front, like relocated features and permission mappings, appeared to reduce anxiety.
  • Personalized onboarding. Initial reactions suggested tailored onboarding helped manage expectations.
  • Feature discoverability. Mapping out the "Where's my..." flows likely reduced confusion about moved or changed features.
  • Permissions and roles. Proactive messaging about permission changes seemed to improve acceptance among admins.
